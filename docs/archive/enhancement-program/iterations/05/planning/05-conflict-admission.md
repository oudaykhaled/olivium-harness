# 05 proposal: declared-conflict admission

**Benefit.** A policy owner declares two exact approved claim revisions incompatible, chooses the governing revision, and receives a specific refusal until a plan reflects it. Detection is manual; goal selection grants no policy authority. Company fitness remains unproven.

**Evidence.** `Evidence.create_claim/1` and `Decisions.decide/2` bind immutable claim hashes to approval. `Company.create_plan/1` snapshots revisions; `decide_plan/2` and `build_inputs/1` recheck eligibility. `Harnesses.build_plan/2`, direct `build/1`, and `eligible/1` cover publication/export. The records schema has `conflict_ids`, but domain code persists no resolution. [03 proposal](../../03/planning/04-declared-conflicts.md) identifies the gap; [04 handoff](../../04/handoff-to-05.md) retains competing priorities.

**One bounded change.** Store workspace-scoped immutable declarations keyed by an unordered pair of distinct claim IDs and approved revision hashes, with actor, reason, digest, and atomic audit. Store one immutable resolution naming the winning revision and expected declaration digest. Reject foreign, stale, superseded, withdrawn, duplicate, or unapproved references. Pin applicable declarations/resolutions in plan snapshots. A selected unresolved claim, both conflicting claims selected, or a selected loser blocks with the conflict ID. A successor selecting only the winner may proceed. Recheck current declarations at approval, planned/direct build, artifact eligibility, and export. Later declarations invalidate affected plans and bindings; historical bytes remain intact. Serialize declaration/admission against the privacy/build fence; extend typed source/workspace purge and restore quarantine.

**Acceptance.** Two contradictory approved fixtures build before declaration. Declaration refuses approval, build, and export with an actionable ID. Exact resolution plus winner-only successor succeeds. Changed hashes, withdrawal, foreign scope, purge/restore, concurrent declaration, and legacy verification have negative tests; unaffected artifacts remain eligible.

**Trade-off and ownership.** Goal-origin approval explains origin but leaves known contradictions admissible; current task identity improves qualification but cannot settle policy. Root owns migration/integration; domain and presentation authors own disjoint code/tests. Existing exports may become ineligible; retain history and give precise refusal.
