# Planner 11 — honest corpus progress and recovery

**Bounded user benefit.** In Company, an operator selects an authorised saved corpus/project and goal, then sees durable, paginated capture-to-retrieval status. A progress card shows eligible files discovered, captured, indexed and retrievable; skipped, stale, blocked and failed files; last checkpoint; and incomplete or unknown coverage. Each stage has its own denominator and timestamp. “Indexed” never means “analysed,” and an unknown discovery total is shown as unknown, not 100%. A bounded failure page exposes reasons and retries only for retryable items. The card leads into evidence and deliberate review on the corpus-to-export journey.

**Source observations.** Frozen `evidence/local_import.ex:3–5,21–41,54–83` gathers at most 500 paths and reads a 16 MiB batch in one `read_all`; it has no per-file checkpoint. `company/analysis.ex:188–229,257–274` authorises profile/project union IDs but sends complete selected bodies under 32 KiB. It rejects stale/inactive sources (`:237–254`). The UI renders every assigned source as a checkbox and static “completeness unknown” text (`company/analysis.ex:180–185,241–285`); its busy notice covers one request (`:303–323`). CompanyPanel keeps an in-memory token (`company_panel.ex:389–428`); tests establish stale selection and late callback/reset refusal (`company_analysis_test.exs:331–350,355–440`). Ledger retains uncertain usage (`models/ledger.ex:44–125,128–184`), but no corpus-stage accounting exists.

**Necessary seams and owners.** Root owns revisioned SQLite corpus-job/read model and migration: server-derived nonempty workspace/project group, stable cursor, bounded page, per-file idempotency key, checkpoint, stage totals/unknowns, retryability and generation. A backend author extends safe capture in bounded batches and adds index/retrieval status while retaining Elixir evidence authority. A UI author owns CompanyPanel and analysis presentation: status card, accessible pagination, labelled retry, goal binding and blocked-model/consent explanation. Graphiti 0.31.2’s bounded Python adapter may report indexed episodes; Elixir validates revisions, scope and serving eligibility. FTS5 is the measured baseline. Resume appears only with durable checkpoint/retry.

**Acceptance.** Positive: a public synthetic corpus larger than the current import batch shows monotonically advancing persisted counts across page/reload, one saved goal and a bounded relevant/contrary evidence preview that leads into exact edit/reject/approve. Negative: foreign scope, revoked or changed revisions, missing model consent and incomplete scan show blocked/unknown coverage and dispatch nothing; no denominator or citation is invented. Recovery: interrupt after a checkpoint, resume without duplicate revisions or double billing, retain failed items, and keep tombstoned derivatives unservable through a failed Graphiti summary rebuild. Verify corrected guidance in actual target exports and representative assessment in the connected follow-on slice.

**Trade-off/dissent.** A spinner plus larger source limit is cheaper but hides partial capture and repeats the whole-corpus failure mode. Full graph-first ingestion offers richer retrieval but delays the operator’s basic answer to “what was covered?” Prefer the smaller durable status seam and measure first-page/p95 latency and critical missed evidence before expanding scale claims. Estimate 4–6 engineering days plus root migration/integration and independent UX/negative-path verification; million-file throughput, real-model quality and novice success remain unproven.
